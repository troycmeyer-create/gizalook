How it works

Three steps.
That's the whole thing.

No training courses. No 90-day onboarding. If you can upload a PDF, you can use Gizalook. Workers are reading steps on their first day — and they set it up in under two minutes.

📤

Upload the drawing

You upload the PDF. Pick the experience level for your crew — first-year through to senior. Hit Generate. Gizalook reads the drawing and creates plain-language steps, Critical Numbers, material specs, and hold points. Takes about 60 seconds.

✅

Review and publish

You review the steps before they go live — add a field note if anything needs a tweak. Hit Publish. Every assigned worker gets the steps on their phone instantly. One drawing. Whole crew. Same understanding. Good as gold.

📱

Crew works through it on site

Big text. Plain language. One step at a time. Tick it off, move on. VERIFY flags show in orange — "Oi — check this with your super before you go further." Stop Work = full-screen block. You see their progress in real time. Plans change? Push a field note. Done.

Adapts to your crew

Same drawing.
Different crew? No worries.

A first-year apprentice and a 10-year leading hand don't need the same level of detail. Gizalook reads the same drawing differently depending on who's holding the phone.

Year 1–2

The Apprentice

Full knowledge layer. Every abbreviation explained. Every step with context. Learns while working — builds plan literacy on the job, not instead of it. Stoked to actually understand what they're doing.

Year 3–4

Getting There

Clear instructions, key context. Dimensions and materials front and centre. Anything ambiguous flagged for a check. Gets it done right without the noise.

Qualified

The Tradesman

Condensed view. Key steps and critical tolerances. Enough detail to nail it, not so much it slows you down. Confirms what you already know, flags what's different on this job.

Senior / Leading Hand

The Boss on the Shovel

Exception-based. Only what's unusual on this drawing. Flags, deviations, hold points. Everything non-standard surfaces automatically. She'll be right — but when she won't be, you'll know.
